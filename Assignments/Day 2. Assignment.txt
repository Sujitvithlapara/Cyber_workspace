Q2. Research and list 3 popular CPU socket types (for example: LGA1151, AM4, LGA1200) and name one compatible CPU model for each.

Ans. 

1. AMD AM4
Platform & Brand: AMD (Pin Grid Array - PGA)

Compatible CPU Example: AMD Ryzen 5 5600X

Overview: A long-standing, highly popular budget and mid-range platform that supported multiple generations of Ryzen processors (Ryzen 1000 through 5000 series) using DDR4 memory.

2. Intel LGA1200
Platform & Brand: Intel (Land Grid Array - LGA)

Compatible CPU Example: Intel Core i7-10700K (also supports 11th Gen processors like the i5-11600K)

Overview: Designed to support Intel's 10th and 11th generation Core desktop processors (Comet Lake and Rocket Lake architectures).

3. Intel LGA1700
Platform & Brand: Intel (Land Grid Array - LGA)

Compatible CPU Example: Intel Core i5-12600K (supports 12th, 13th, and 14th Gen Intel Core processors)

Overview: A widely adopted mainstream socket that introduced support for performance/efficient core architectures and compatibility with both DDR4 and DDR5 memory depending on the motherboard choice.

Q3. Explain in your own words the primary functions of the CPU and the chipset on a motherboard, and describe how they work together when you open a music app like Spotify.

Ans.

1. Primary Functions of the CPU
The CPU (Central Processing Unit) is often referred to as the "brain" of the computer. Its primary responsibility is to execute instructions and process data from the operating system and software applications.

Instruction Execution: It fetches, decodes, and executes program instructions (using the Fetch-Decode-Execute cycle).

Arithmetic and Logic: It performs complex mathematical calculations and logical comparisons via its Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU).

Data Coordination: It acts as the central coordinator, telling other components what to do and when to do it based on user commands.

2. Primary Functions of the Chipset
The chipset (often called the Platform Controller Hub or PCH in modern systems) is the "traffic controller" of the motherboard. While the CPU handles raw processing power, the chipset manages how data moves between the CPU, system memory (RAM), storage drives, and external peripherals.

I/O Management: It controls communication paths for USB ports, SATA drives, audio controllers, and expansion slots (PCIe).

Data Routing: It ensures that data from your keyboard, mouse, storage drives, and network cards reaches the CPU efficiently without bottlenecks.

Power Management: It helps regulate power states and system sleep/wake cycles.

3. Working Together: Opening Spotify
When you click on an app like Spotify, the CPU and chipset coordinate a precise sequence of events to load and play your music:

Input Detection: When you double-click the Spotify icon, the mouse click travels via USB to the chipset. The chipset registers the signal and immediately notifies the CPU.

Fetching Data from Storage: The CPU decides to launch Spotify and tells the chipset to retrieve the application files from your SSD or hard drive. The chipset opens the storage communication lane and pulls the data.

Staging in RAM: The data streamed from the storage drive passes through the chipset and is loaded into the RAM (system memory) so the CPU can access it quickly.

Processing and Launching: The CPU reads the code from RAM, executes the startup instructions, builds the user interface, and displays the Spotify window on your monitor.

Playing Audio: When you click play on a song, the CPU downloads compressed audio packets from the internet (via the network controller managed by the chipset), decodes the compressed digital stream into raw audio data, and sends it through the chipset to your motherboard's audio chip to output sound to your speakers or headphones.

Q4. Find a clear photo of a real motherboard (from the internet or your own device), and identify at least 5 different ports or slots by circling them and labeling each one.

Ans. 

1.RAM Slots (DIMM Slots): Located near the upper section of the board (the four long alternating blue and black slots). These are where dual-channel memory modules are installed.

2.CPU Socket: Positioned toward the middle-right area, featuring the metallic retention bracket and latch mechanism where the processor is securely mounted.

3.PCIe x16 Expansion Slots: Found across the lower-middle portion of the board (the long black slots). These are primarily used for adding dedicated graphics cards or high-bandwidth expansion cards.

4.SATA Ports: Situated at the bottom-right corner of the board (the small white and orange blocks). These connectors link traditional storage drives like mechanical hard drives or 2.5-inch SSDs to the system.

5.Rear I/O Panel: Located on the far left edge of the board. This block houses all the external connectors facing the back of the computer case, such as USB ports, network (LAN) ports, and video output jacks.
